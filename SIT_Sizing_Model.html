Method

How the estimate is calculated

cases = Σ counted driver × cases per driver (by complexity) + business rules: critical in full, standard credited by rule unit tests, × mutation × variants + workflows: transitions × (1 + invalid share) + roles: (protected actions + role-gated transitions) × roles × allow/deny × sampling, + segregation of duties + field validation (fields × field-type matrix, after shift-left credit) + mapping + channel and language extras hours = Σ cases × [ design min + exec min × (cycles + regression × (1 − automated)) × (1 + retest) × environment ] ÷ 60 × (1 + overhead) FTE = hours ÷ ( weeks × days per week × productive hours × blended capacity ) range = FTE × (1 ± band) band: rough ±40% · detailed ±15%
  1. Rough and detailed use the same formula. Rough fills the driver counts from feature size proxies and uses the longer design time per case, because there is no FSD to design from.
  2. Field validation is credited by coverage. At 100% the team proved it in L1/L2, so SIT runs one sample per screen (frontend) or per API (backend). At 50–99% half the field-type matrix comes back into SIT; below 50% all of it does, about 12 cases per field per layer for a typical mix. Validation cases use their own lighter effort rates and one full pass, because they are templated and data-driven.
  3. A mutation score below the gate means logic defects reach SIT. Business-rule cases go up and the retest rate rises.
  4. Roles use a role × guarded-action matrix with risk-based sampling, never roles × every case. Guarded actions are the protected actions outside workflows plus the role-gated workflow transitions. Each approval workflow adds segregation-of-duties cases.
  5. Business rules split into critical and standard. Critical rules always run the full decision table in SIT. Standard rules are credited by business rule unit tests: one wiring case each when proven, half the table at 50–99%, the full table below 50%. Rules that vary by product, segment or currency are sampled pairwise.
  6. Each extra channel or language adds a sampled share of screen and journey cases, not a full copy of the suite. Environment availability stretches execution time. Regression automation shrinks only the regression pass; building and maintaining that automation is not counted here.
  7. Mutation testing itself is never counted as SIT work. It is automated and owned by the delivery team; it only changes how much SIT has to repeat.

Basis

Standards and rationale

Defaults are heuristics

No global standard prescribes numbers such as cases per API or minutes per case. Standards define the methods; the numbers come from your own history. This model follows recognised methods and labels every number as a heuristic until a team calibrates it.

How to calibrate, using the ISTQB techniques

  1. Ratio-based: run this model on one or two completed projects and compare it with their actual cases and hours. Adjust cases per driver until it lands within range.
  2. Extrapolation: after the first SIT cycle, replace execution minutes per case, retest rate and productive hours with what the team measured.
  3. Three-point: where there is no data yet, ask three senior testers for optimistic (a), most likely (m) and pessimistic (b) minutes per case and use E = (a + 4m + b) ÷ 6.
  4. Wideband Delphi: agree cases per driver across QE leads in short rounds, then export the result as the shared calibration profile.

Wording for a Biz Case or QBR

The case-count method follows ISO/IEC/IEEE 29119-4 test design techniques and ISTQB ratio-based estimation. The effort structure is modelled on TMap Test Point Analysis, and the ranges follow the cone of uncertainty. The numeric defaults are uncalibrated heuristics and will be replaced with actuals after the first SIT cycle.
